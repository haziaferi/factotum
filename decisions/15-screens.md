**Navigation at a glance (2026-10-05).** The owner found A+ and A++ crowded: "good for clarity of choice, difficult to parse through at a glance". They asked whether colour and depth help, and whether A+ can be combined with P7. `tools/screens/nav_glance.py` tries every combination of four levers, 144 variants: the bar, the ⊞ form (including P7's grid inside ⊞), the top row, and the coding (plain, hue, or depth). Each variant is rendered in light and dark and its clutter measured from pixels; the step costs come from `nav_score2`. The tables are in `docs/screens/t0/nav-glance.md` and the mockups in `nav-glance.html`. The findings:
- **Depth carries it.** Depth means tonal surfaces instead of rules, with inactive items receded. For the same structure it cuts measured clutter by about 40% (0.063 to 0.038 for A+), and it does so in 48 of 48 pairs at all four edge thresholds tried. It also brings the elements at full contrast down from 12 to 3, at no step cost.
- **Hue alone does nothing** measurable. It pays only inside ⊞, when each module is a filled card in its own hue (6.0 to 4.0 items inspected, in the search model). It constrains Q4: equal-lightness module hues, at most 6.
- **P7 inside ⊞ trades reach for calm.** The module grid is the least cluttered but reaches 25 screens in two taps, not 29. Folders (3 shortcuts per module) sit between. **P7 as a tab** costs 59 more steps a week than A+ and reaches only 7 screens in two taps: P7's back and forth, measured.
- **The top row** trades taps for elements. Trimming it to 3 plus More saves one element and costs half a tap per sibling switch.

**Correction (owner, same day): the keywords are single candidates to discuss, not a solution.** The first study (`palette_t0.py`, `palette.html`) blended each module's keywords into one hue on three shells. That was premature, and it is kept only as a record. The candidates are now shown one keyword at a time, each with the lexicon's own named pairs, unblended, on a snippet of its module, by `tools/screens/palette_candidates.py` (`docs/screens/t0/palette-candidates.html` and `.md`). The script refuses to run if a hex is missing from its attributed shard, and designkit measures the ratios. **Open: which candidates the owner keeps, per module.**

**Adapted to the shared ground (owner, same day).** The owner asked to keep the mockups' ground, `#F4F4F2` light and `#17181A` dark, and to adapt every other colour to it with color-optimization-core. `tools/screens/palette_adapt.py` writes `palette-adapted.html` and `.md`. Each lexicon pair runs through designkit's refine search with the ground pinned, on the optimization-engines portfolio. Three spec patches are stated in its docstring:
- the pinned ground gets a 0.01 width, because the portfolio refuses a zero-width range;
- the fill envelopes are widened to include each keyword's own colours;
- roles the keyword does not declare are left out of the fidelity budget.

A minimal repair gives the portfolio a feasible seed, lightness first. Hue moves only where designkit's accent_distinct asks for it, split between primary and accent. Results: **52 of 52** palettes meet every constraint (text ≥ 4.5:1, fills carry a label at 4.5:1 and stand dE ≥ 10 off the ground, drift ≤ dE 15 from the lexicon). Each keyword stays a separate candidate.

**Compatible groups (owner, same day, Pages left out).** `tools/screens/palette_groups.py` scores all 180 groups of one adapted pair per module, writing `palette-groups.html` and `.md`. Each module is identified by its palette's primary, judged on the shared ground in both modes.
- **The floor:** CIEDE2000 ≥ 10 between the three module colours, with normal vision and under simulated deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia (color-optimization-core's floor, calibrated on Okabe–Ito). **58 groups pass.**
- **Corrected twice the same day:**
  - The owner wants **groups of three palettes shown whole and apart, not merged** into one screen.
  - **"As many as can be created", with no limit of 4:** every compatible trio is a group, and a palette may be in several. Only palettes that fit no trio are excluded, and there are none.
  - The page shows all **58 groups**, ordered by harmony (designkit's template fit), with balance and colour-blind margin beside each. Filters list the groups a given palette is in.

**Palettes (Q4), second slate (2026-10-06).** The owner clarified that a palette is a full set used across a module's screens, not a few accents for Today.

The owner's answers:
- Roles are primary, secondary and tertiary, a container for each, and chart colours. Grounds stay neutral.
- One shared state set covers done, skipped, due soon and attention. Its colours appear nowhere else.
- Chart colours are scored: fixed lists against Chronicle's picker, keeping in mind that there are more than 8 activities. The Notion problem must be avoided.
- The keywords are the old picks plus fresh ones, up to 50 groups.
- Purples and indigos are tertiary, except in a few groups.
- The groups are shown as an overview with expandable screens.

What was measured:
- **Chart colours** (`series.md`, `tools/screens/series_contest.py`). A guided picker beats fixed lists of 3 to 8 and Chronicle's picker as built. Its colours sit at two lightness tiers, readable in each theme, with reserved and forbidden hues removed. It suggests the colour farthest from every colour in use, marks colours already taken, and labels chart lines directly. With 24 activities it keeps every colour unique, readable and colour-blind-legible when the suggestions are accepted. So module palettes carry only a chart scale; item colours come from the picker.
- **State set** (`states.md`, plus a sweep in `module-palettes.md`). Done, skipped and due soon are drawn in ink, and attention in magenta. The ochre first proposed for due soon took away the clay and sand family that keeps routines apart from blue calendars.
- **Module palettes** (`module-palettes.md` / `.html`, `tools/screens/module_palettes.py`). 50 palettes pass (Calendar 17, Routines 17, check-in 16); 145 groups are feasible; **12 can be chosen under the owner's caps**. Most feasible groups contain a pink-led or purple-led palette. The sweep table shows how many groups each relaxation would allow. **Open: whether to relax a cap, and the shortlist.**
